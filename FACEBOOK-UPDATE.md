UPDATE - A SECOND SCAM IS NOW GOING AROUND (fake Services Australia / PRODA)

A colleague forwarded me two of these this week, so adding it here.

The email claims your PRODA account is no longer linked to HPOS and needs to be "re-linked", with a Reconnect PRODA button. It looks like a genuine automated notice from Services Australia.

What gives it away: the button does not go to a .gov.au address. In the ones I have seen it goes to verifications .es, a Spanish website. Worse, the text in the email that reads "servicesaustralia.gov.au/proda" is also a link to that same fake site - so reading the words and trusting them is exactly the trap. The two copies came from ghaspert .com and machsselbst .com (spaces added so nobody can click).

This one is different from the file-share scam. It does not install anything on your computer. It steals your PRODA login, and PRODA is the front door to HPOS - Medicare claiming, DVA, the immunisation register and patient records. So the damage is fraudulent billing under your provider number and a patient data exposure.

THE RULE: a real Services Australia link always ends in .gov.au. Never reach PRODA through a button in an email. Type the address yourself or use your own bookmark. And if you want to check with them, look their phone number up yourself - this scam prints its own fake "Services Australia" number in the email.

IF SOMEONE AT YOUR PRACTICE ENTERED THEIR PRODA DETAILS:

1. Change the PRODA password now, from a different device, going to the site yourself rather than through any link.
2. Check PRODA for changes you did not make - delegates, linked organisations, changed contact details. Adding themselves as a delegate is how they keep access after you change the password.
3. Check HPOS for claims you did not submit and for changed bank details.
4. Phone Services Australia on a number you looked up yourself and report the account as compromised.
5. Treat it as a patient data incident, not just a billing one, and call your indemnity insurer.

Full checklist, and a step-by-step tool that asks you a couple of questions and gives you exactly what to do, at phishwatch.parkrddental.com.au

Two other things while I am updating:

- The free browser extension on the site now flags this PRODA scam as well as the file-share one, and I have added several more scam web addresses that have turned up since I first posted.
- Correction to my original post: I said these emails tend to arrive at 3am. That was true of the first one I saw, but of the samples I have now collected, most actually arrived during normal business hours. Do not use the time of day to judge an email - a sensible looking timestamp means nothing.
